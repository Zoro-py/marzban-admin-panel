# Product Brief (for Seyed Product Advisor)

## What
Two products sharing one backend, sitting on top of a Marzban VPN panel.

1. **The reseller dashboard** (`frontend/`, `bot/`) — the operator's own tool.
   Tracks who owns which VPN account, who owes what, auto-renews and auto-bills
   accounts as they run low, settles pay-as-you-go groups.
2. **The customer shop** (`shopbot/`) — a Telegram bot where a consumer buys a
   subscription for themselves: pick a volume, pay by card-to-card transfer,
   upload a receipt photo, wait for the operator to approve, receive a
   subscription link and QR code.

## Who
- **Product 1:** one person — a VPN reseller running a Marzban panel, selling
  to individuals, families and small offices. They handle money by hand today.
- **Product 2:** Iranian consumers who want a working VPN on their phone. Mostly
  non-technical. They find the bot through word of mouth or a channel link.

## Stage
Product 1 is live and in daily use. Product 2 was built this week and has never
had a real customer through it.

## Pin Problem
**Product 1:** a reseller's money lives in their head. Who paid, who owes, whose
account is about to run out — all of it is manual, and getting it wrong costs
real money in both directions.

**Product 2 (the one under review):** buying a VPN in Iran is an act of trust
with no safety net. There is no payment gateway a small reseller can use, no
escrow, no refund mechanism, no app store rating to check. The buyer must send
money to a stranger's personal bank card, photograph the receipt, and then wait
— with nothing to hold — for a human to notice and approve it. Then, if it
works, they receive a URL they have to import into an app they may not have.

The friction is not the software. The friction is **asking a stranger to pay
first and trust completely, before anything has been proven.**

## Business Model
Resale margin. The operator buys panel capacity and sells subscriptions on top.
Product 2 is the attempt to turn a manual, one-conversation-at-a-time business
into something that runs without the operator being present for every sale.

## Competitors / Alternatives
- Dozens of other Telegram VPN shops, most with identical flows and identical
  card-to-card payment. Switching cost for the buyer is effectively zero.
- Buying directly from a friend who resells.
- Free VPNs, which mostly do not work in Iran but cost nothing to try.

## Why Now
The operator already has the panel, the accounts and the customers. What they
don't have is a way to sell without personally answering every message. Every
sale currently consumes their attention, so the business cannot grow past the
number of conversations one person can hold in a day.

## Open Questions
- The flow currently makes a first-time buyer fund a wallet **before** they can
  choose what to buy. Is a stored-value wallet the right model at all for
  someone buying their first subscription, or is it a second-purchase feature
  that has been put in front of the first purchase?
- There is no trial and no proof-of-service before payment. In a market where
  the buyer has been scammed before, what is the minimum credible thing to give
  away?
- Approval is manual and has no stated wait time. How much does that silence
  actually cost, versus the risk of auto-approving payments?
- Pricing is purely linear (one price per GB, one duration). There is no reason
  for anyone to buy more than the minimum.
